The Award Boards: Two Runaways, Three Real Fights

AUGUST 27, 2026

Our MVP, Cy Young and Rookie Scores — who leads each race, and by how much.

Late August is when the awards talk stops being idle. By our 0-100 Scores, two names have pulled clear — Yordan Alvarez in the AL and Jacob Misiorowski for the NL Cy Young — while the other four races are genuine scraps. Here's who's actually leading, and by how much.

MVP watch

Our MVP Score (0–100) — each hitter’s OPS, on-base, power, run production and playing time graded against the league average, then blended. Higher is better.

Alvarez owns the AL at 85.2, and it isn't close — his 1.051 OPS is a full tier above the field, with 36 homers and a .322 average to match; Ben Rice (77.5) and Junior Caminero (76.7) are fighting over second. The NL is the tight one: James Wood leads at 78.2 on a .928 OPS for Washington, but Pete Crow-Armstrong is right on him at 77.5, and unlike Wood's cold stretch, Crow-Armstrong is still humming at a .909 clip over his last 30 days.

Cy Young watch

Our Cy Young Score (0–100) — ERA, WHIP, strikeout and walk rates, workload and quality starts, each graded against the league average, then blended. Higher is better.

Misiorowski is the runaway of the whole edition — an 89.3 Score built on a 1.68 ERA and a 0.76 WHIP, better than Chris Sale (80.8) can reach even with his 2.20 ERA. The AL is far friendlier: Cam Schlittler paces it at 80.7 with 193 strikeouts, but Dylan Cease (72.9) and Drew Rasmussen (72.5) are all but tied a step behind.

Rookie of the Year watch

Our Rookie Score (0–100) — the MVP and Cy Young engines applied to first-year players, hitters and pitchers ranked on one scale. Higher is better.

Munetaka Murakami tops the AL rookies at 72.0, his 29 homers doing the heavy lifting even as the average sits at .219, with Cleveland's Parker Messick (70.6) and his 2.50 ERA close behind. In the NL, Sal Stewart leads at 70.9 — a rookie with 100 RBI and 28 homers for Cincinnati, and the clearest of the three rookie front-runners.
